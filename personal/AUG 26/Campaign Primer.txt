The Tale of the First Sundering
A story every child in the kingdom grows up hearing.
No one agrees on exactly how it happened.
Some say the stars vanished.
Some say the moon turned crimson.
Others claim the earth itself cried out.
What every version agrees upon is this:
Over a thousand years ago, the world nearly ended.

In those ancient days, strange tears opened across the land.
The sky split apart as though made of glass, revealing another world beyond. Through those impossible openings came creatures unlike anything the people of the kingdom had ever seen.
They were called the Shreeden.
Every painting and carving depicts them differently. Some show them as tall, glowing figures with enormous eyes. Others portray them as winged spirits or ghostly beings wrapped in blue light. No one knows which, if any, are accurate.
The old stories insist they were not invaders.
They were fleeing.
Behind them came horrors from beyond the tears—monsters so terrible that many legends refuse to describe them directly. Some tales say they devoured cities. Others claim they erased people so completely that even their loved ones forgot they had ever existed.
Whatever the truth, the people of the kingdom stood beside the Shreeden.
Together they fought a war unlike any before or since.
The battles are said to have lasted for weeks, with armies defending every great city while scholars, mystics, and kings searched desperately for a way to close the wounds in the world.
In the end, the enemy was driven back.
The tears were sealed.
The kingdom was saved.

Then the Shreeden disappeared.
Some stories claim they sacrificed themselves to mend the broken world.
Others say they returned to their own realm once the danger had passed.
Still others insist they ascended into the heavens as guardians watching over the world from beyond mortal sight.
No one knows the truth.

From those events grew the Faith of the Veil, one of the oldest religious traditions in the kingdom.
Its priests teach that the First Sundering truly happened and that the sacrifice of the Shreeden should never be forgotten. They preserve ancient songs, ceremonies, and relics said to date back to that forgotten age.
Though the faith remains respected, many people quietly wonder whether its stories are history or simply beautiful myths that have grown larger with each generation.
Scholars argue endlessly.
Some believe the First Sundering was a real historical event whose details have become exaggerated over a thousand years.
Others insist there is no evidence it ever happened at all.
After so many centuries, the line between history and legend has blurred beyond recognition.

Today, life goes on.
Farmers tend their fields.
Merchants travel the roads.
Children still hear stories of glowing beings who once saved the world before vanishing forever.
Most people think of the Shreeden no differently than dragons, giants, or the heroes of old epics—figures from another age whose stories carry lessons, even if they never truly existed.
The kingdom faces more immediate concerns now.
For years, fighting has continued along the northern border. Officially, the crown says hostile forces threaten the frontier, and every season more soldiers are sent to defend the realm. News from the border is often confusing—villages abandoned, patrols disappearing, strange accounts from frightened survivors—but such tales are easily dismissed as exaggerations born from the hardships of war.
For ordinary people, these are simply the troubles of the age. The ancient stories of the Shreeden belong to the past, told around hearth fires on cold nights or recited during holy festivals.
If there is any truth to those legends, it has long since been lost to time. Or so everyone believes.
